Summary:
The “Platteville Button” design was developed by Nick Shaw. It came about as part of an optional assignment of the Engineering Physics Systems course at the University of Wisconsin Platteville, taught by Dr. Harold T. Evensen; the course serves as an introduction to the Engineering Physics Department and coursework, while teaching PLC and microcontroller programming.The “Platteville Button” is designed to connect to electronic toys that are adapted with a 3.5mm female mono speaker jack, for users with special needs. The point of the “Platteville Button” project was to create a cost-effective, simple, and widely usable button that was able to be manufactured by families or organizations to serve as an alternative to the commercial buttons. The button can also be created in-house by UW-Platteville students or faculty and distributed with adapted "Toy Hack" toys as needed. The exact cost of the Platteville Button depends on the exact materials used and the quantity being produced, but the average cost to create a few is generally well under $10 per unit.It is important to note that this button utilizes a simple circuit board to assist in the electrical connections. We describe two options for makers:(1) A home-made “circuit board,” which requires a CNC Mill or Router and some knowledge in CAD and CAM to manufacture. A .step file of the circuit board has been included in the files, and notes and pointers for making the boards are below.(2) Ordering a pre-cut printed circuit board (PCB) from on online vendor. The files needed for upload, and detailed instructions, are below. Depending on quantities ordered, the cost is &lt;$1 each.The documents include (1) a description of the fabrication process [.PDF]; (2) files for 3D printing (including three button sizes) [.STL]; (3) file for the circuit board insert [.STEP]; (4) file for ordering prefabricated circuit board inserts [.ZIP]See the Assembly Instructions for notes and pointers on making the board insert, including a more in-depth explanation of how the button itself functions.
